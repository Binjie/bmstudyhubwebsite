Thinking Test

Logic and Pattern Reasoning Check

A short reasoning check for patterns, analogies, observation, and problem decomposition.

Updated
June 2026
Time
10-15 minutes
Audience
Upper primary and middle school

What It Measures

This test focuses on four thinking habits:

  • noticing details before answering
  • finding patterns across numbers, shapes, or short stories
  • explaining why an answer makes sense
  • breaking a large problem into smaller steps

Sample Question Style

Students might see a sequence of numbers, a grid of shapes, or a short real-life situation. Instead of only choosing an answer, they explain the rule they noticed.

Example: A student sees 2, 6, 12, 20, 30 and describes the change between terms before predicting the next number.

How To Use Results

Use the result to decide what kind of thinking support the student needs next. A student who spots patterns quickly may need harder explanation tasks. A student who guesses quickly may need practice slowing down and checking evidence.

How To Use

Treat the result as a conversation starter.

A test can reveal useful patterns, but it should not become a fixed label. Compare results with classroom observation, reflection, and student voice.
