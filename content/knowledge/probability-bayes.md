## The Core Idea

Probability does not promise what will happen once. It describes how often something tends to happen across many similar situations.

This is why a 70% chance of rain can still produce a sunny afternoon. The forecast was not automatically wrong; it was describing uncertainty.

## Life Example

A test with high accuracy can still produce surprising results when the event is rare.

If only a very small group truly has a condition, even a good test may create many false positives. This is the doorway into base-rate thinking: new evidence should be interpreted together with how common the event was to begin with.

## Classroom Example

Use colored cards in a bag. Change the number of red and blue cards, then ask students to predict what a test result means before and after they know the base rate.
